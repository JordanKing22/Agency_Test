# Objection handling

The pattern for every objection: **agree, ask, answer, re-ask.** Don't argue. Find the real concern with a
question, answer that one, then ask for the decision again.

---

### "We already have an answering service."
> "Good, so you already know after-hours calls matter. Two questions: does it book the job into your schedule,
> or take a message? And how long until someone calls the homeowner back?"

The answer is almost always "takes a message, we call back in the morning." Then:
> "That gap is where they call the next company. The dispatcher books the job on the call, sends real
> emergencies to on-call immediately, and costs about what a busy answering service does. Want to run it on
> overflow for 60 days next to what you have and compare?"

### "I (or my CSR) answer every call."
> "That's rare and it shows in your reviews. When you're on a job, or your CSR is on another line on the first
> cold Monday, where does call number two go?"

Offer the free proof: "Let's look at last week's missed and abandoned calls in your phone system. If it's near
zero, you don't need me and I'll tell you so."

### "AI will annoy my customers / it'll sound like a robot."
> "That was my worry too. Call it right now and try to trip it up."

Then: it says it's a virtual assistant up front, hands off to a person any time someone asks, and starts on
after-hours and overflow only, so your team still takes the daytime calls. "Compare it to voicemail, not to your
best CSR, because voicemail is what those callers get today."

### "It's too expensive." / "I don't have the budget."
> "Compared to what?"

Then use their numbers: "You said a replacement is about {$11,000}. If this books one extra replacement in a
year, it's paid for itself. Is the worry the price, or whether it'll actually book that one job?"

If it's really cash flow: lead with the sprint ("5 appointments or you pay nothing"), or start the dispatcher on
after-hours only.

### "Send me some information."
> "Happy to. So I send the right thing, what would you need to see to say yes?"

Send exactly that plus the demo number, and book a 10-minute follow-up before you hang up: "Can we look at it
together Thursday at 7:30 so I can answer questions?"

### "I've been burned by marketing agencies."
> "Most HVAC owners have. Can I ask what happened?"

Listen. Then: "We're not selling leads, clicks or SEO. We work only with calls you're already getting and quotes
you already gave. And the sprint is guaranteed: 5 booked appointments or you don't pay a cent. Your risk is
the export, which takes 5 minutes."

### "I need to talk to my partner / spouse / GM."
> "Of course. What do you think they'll ask?"

Answer it now. Then: "Let's get 15 minutes with both of you. Would tomorrow at 7:30 or 4:30 work?" Never let
the owner pitch it for you.

### "We're slammed right now, call me after the season."
> "That's exactly when this pays for itself. Busy is when calls get missed. Setup takes about 2 hours of your
> team's time spread over a week, and we do the rest. If we wait until spring, you've already lost the winter."

### "We use ServiceTitan / Podium / Housecall, and it already does follow-up."
> "Nice, then setup is easier. Out of curiosity, how many of last year's unsold quotes got a follow-up in the last
> 30 days?"

Usually the tool is there and nobody runs it. "We run it for you and we're on the hook for results."

### "How do I know it'll work?"
> "You shouldn't take my word for it. That's why the sprint is guaranteed and the dispatcher has to pay for itself in
> 60 days or you get your setup fee back. And you've already called the demo."

### "My customers don't want texts / we don't text people."
> "Totally fine. We only text customers who opted in, and the campaign works with email and phone follow-up
> alone. Do your service agreements have a texting consent box? If not, we'll add one going forward."

### "What if it books a job wrong / promises something?"
> "It only books job types and slots you approve, never quotes prices beyond the fees you give it, and sends
> anything unusual to a person. We test 20 scenarios before go-live and review calls weekly. In the first month you
> get a text summary of every call."

### "Not right now. Call me in spring."
> "Will do. Quick question before I go: spring is when the AC rush starts, and winter is when furnaces die at
> 2am. Which of those do you want answered?" If still no: `bookedout call <id> --outcome callback --note
> "spring"`, set a reminder for March, and put them in the heat-wave sequence then.
