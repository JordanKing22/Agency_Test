# 1. The niche: commercial specialty subcontractors

"Specialty contractors" covers every trade that isn't the general contractor: drywall, electrical, concrete,
glazing, fire protection, painting, flooring, roofing, masonry, insulation and so on. That splits into two very
different businesses:

- **Residential specialty** (roofers, remodelers, fence and pool companies) sell to homeowners. They're buried in
  agencies selling Facebook leads and ads, and the best fast offer for them (chasing old estimates) is what the
  HVAC agency in this repo already does.
- **Commercial specialty subcontractors** sell to *general contractors*. Their revenue comes from invitations to
  bid (ITBs). Almost no agency serves them well, because homeowner lead gen is useless to them.

**Decision:** sell to commercial specialty subs with 10-200 employees, in Divisions 03-09 and 21-26 (concrete,
masonry, openings, finishes, fire protection, electrical), in metros that publish building permit data.

**What we sell them:** more invitations to bid from the GCs actually building in their market, and follow-up on
every bid so more of them turn into awarded work. No AI receptionist, no homeowner leads, no ads.

## Why the offer works

**GCs want more subs.** On every bid, a GC needs enough sub prices per trade to be competitive ("coverage"). A
qualified sub asking to be added to the bid list is usually welcome. That's why the front-end guarantee (10 new GC
bid lists in 30 days) is something you control.

**Nobody follows up after bid day.** Estimators move straight to the next takeoff. Whether the GC won the project,
where the number landed, and who's running buyout often go unasked. A follow-up desk turns that gap into awards.

**The data is public.** City permit records show which GCs pulled the most commercial permits in the last 90 days.
That gives you a specific, true first line for every cold email and a free audit ("6 of the top 7 GCs in Austin
don't invite you") that sells the sprint by itself.

**It's B2B.** No homeowner texting, no consumer TCPA exposure, no AI voice. Compliance is cold-email basics.

## Ideal client profile (what the engine scores for)

- Commercial specialty sub, 10-200 employees, owner plus 1-4 estimators
- Website talks about general contractors, tenant improvements, healthcare/education/industrial work, not homeowners
- Certified (MBE, WBE, DBE, SBE, SDVOSB, HUBZone, 8(a)) is a big plus: GCs need certified subs for participation goals
- Hiring (busy now, nervous about next year's backlog)
- Bids mostly to the same 5-10 GCs, doesn't follow up after bid day
- In a metro with open permit data and steady commercial construction

Not a fit: residential-only shops, national subs with their own business development teams, subs with no
bonding/insurance capacity to take on new GCs.

## Trades to start with

Start with trades that bid almost entirely through GCs and exist in every market:

1. **Drywall / framing / acoustical ceilings** (Division 09): many firms, bid-driven, interiors on every job
2. **Electrical** (Division 26): big packages, every project
3. **Glazing / storefront / curtain wall** (Division 08): fewer competitors per market, GCs often short on coverage
4. **Fire protection** (Division 21): required on most commercial work, specialized
5. **Concrete** (Division 03), **painting / flooring** (Division 09)

## Where to start geographically

Pick metros that publish permits with contractor names and have steady commercial activity. Good candidates to
check first: Austin, Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, Phoenix, Denver, Seattle, Nashville, Charlotte, Raleigh,
Atlanta, Chicago, Columbus, Kansas City, Salt Lake City, Orlando, Tampa. Confirm each city's dataset has a
contractor-name column before you commit (`fullbacklog permits` warns you if it doesn't).

**Exclusivity:** one client per trade per metro. It's a selling point ("your competitors can't hire us") and it keeps
you clear of conflicts between clients bidding the same jobs.
