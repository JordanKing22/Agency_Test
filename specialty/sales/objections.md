# Objection handling

Pattern: **agree, ask, answer, re-ask.** Find the real concern with a question, answer that one, ask for the
decision again.

---

### "We have plenty of work right now."
> "Good. How far out does that go?"

Most commercial subs are full for a quarter or two and unsure after that. "The jobs that fill next summer are being
priced this winter. If you get on the lists now, the invitations show up when you need them."

### "We get invited to everything already."
> "Then the audit will be quick. Let's go down the top 10 in {market}. If you're on all of them, I'll tell you not to
> hire us."

This rarely survives the first five names.

### "We only bid for GCs we know. New GCs don't pay / are risky."
> "That's smart. Who's on your no-bid list and why?"

Then: "We screen GCs before we pitch you to them: we look at their permit history, project types and size, and you
approve the target list. You're never obligated to bid anything that comes in; the Bid Desk actually helps you say no
faster."

### "It's too expensive."
> "Compared to what?"

Then with their numbers: "One awarded job at your average of {$120k} at a {15%} margin is {$18k} of gross profit.
The sprint is {$2,500} and guaranteed. Is the worry the price, or whether it'll get you on the lists?"

### "Can't my estimator just do this?"
> "Could they? Sure. Do they have time between takeoffs to research 80 GCs, find their estimators, fill out prequals
> and call every GC after bid day?"

Then: "We cost less than a quarter of a BD hire, and your estimators go back to estimating."

### "Send me the list and I'll handle it."
> "Happy to send it. It's yours either way."

Then: "What usually happens is the list sits in an inbox, because the work is the 80 emails, the prequals, and the
follow-up. If in 30 days you haven't gotten to it, call me."

Book the follow-up call before hanging up.

### "We tried BuildingConnected / PlanHub and got junk ITBs."
> "That's usually the profile, not the platform. What trades and regions were you set up for?"

Then: "Part of the sprint is fixing those profiles so ITBs match your trade, size and area, and the Bid Desk filters
what's left so your estimators only see jobs worth pricing."

### "GCs just use us to check numbers. We never win."
> "That happens. Do you know which GCs award you and which only shop your number?"

Then: "That's the scorecard we build. Within a couple of months you'll know exactly who to stop bidding for, which
frees estimating time for GCs who actually award."

### "We're union / open shop, so a lot of GCs won't work with us."
> "Good to know, that's exactly what we filter on. We'd only pitch you to GCs who use {union/open-shop} subs for
> {trade} on the kind of work you do."

### "I need to talk to my partner / chief estimator."
> "Of course. What do you think they'll ask?" Answer it, then: "Let's get 15 minutes with both of you. I'll bring the
> audit. Tomorrow at 7 or 3:45?"

### "Who else do you work with?"
Founding stage: "We're new to {market}, so I won't show you made-up logos. That's why the sprint is guaranteed and
founding clients pay $1,500. And it's one sub per trade per metro, so if you take it, I can't do this for your
competitors here."

### "Not right now. Call me in January."
> "Will do. One thing first: the GCs pricing spring work are building their sub lists over the next few weeks. Want me
> to at least send you the audit so you can see where you stand?"

Log it: `fullbacklog call <id> --outcome callback --note "January"`.
