House rules

What your dispatcher will and won't do

Compare it to voicemail, because voicemail is what those callers get today.

Always

  • Says it's your virtual assistant at the start of the call
  • Hands off to a person whenever a caller asks
  • Puts safety first: gas smell or a CO alarm means get out and call 911, then it pages on-call
  • Reads back names, phone numbers and addresses

Never

  • Quotes a price you haven't approved
  • Promises a time outside the booked window
  • Pretends to be a person
  • Texts a customer who hasn't opted in

Does it work with ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro or Jobber?

Yes. We book into your scheduling system where it allows it, or into a synced calendar your CSR sees, with full notes on every job.

Do we need a new phone number?

No. Your main number stays. We forward after-hours and overflow calls to the dispatcher, and you can change the rules any time.

What if a customer wants a real person?

They get one. During business hours it transfers to your office. After hours it takes a message, and real emergencies go straight to your on-call tech.

Is there a long contract?

The sprint is one-time. The dispatcher has a 90-day first term, then it's month to month.

What does the sprint need from us?

A 5-minute export of unsold estimates, a 30-minute kickoff call, and someone to run the appointments we book. It works best with 250+ unsold quotes from the last two years.

Is it legal to text our customers?

We only text customers who agreed to receive texts, from a registered number, with an opt-out on every message. Everyone else gets email and phone follow-up.
